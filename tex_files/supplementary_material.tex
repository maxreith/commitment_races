\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-theo:GamePayoffs}}\label{sec:prooftheo1}
Consider the simplified $2\times2$ symmetric normal form game from Table~\ref{M-tab:general2x2}.
Let $A_{1}$ denote the strategy where a player makes a commitment to $A_1$, and let $A_{2}$ denote the strategy where a player commits to $A_2$. $W$ denotes the strategy of waiting.
To prove Proposition~\ref{M-theo:GamePayoffs}, we will derive the symmetric equilibria of the commitment variants of all symmetric $2\times2$ games and compare them to the symmetric equilibria of the base game. We will go through the games one by one.
Note that in these equilibria, if a player waits, she will best respond to any commitment the other player makes. If both players wait, they will play the MSNE of the base game which yields expected payoff $\tfrac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam+\cparam-1}$.


\paragraph{Battle of the Sexes ($\bparam>1$ and $\cparam>1$)}
The commitment Battle of the Sexes does not have symmetric equilibria in pure strategies. We therefore only focus on mixed equilibria here. %edit
If player $-i$ mixes between $A_1$, $A_2$ and $W$ with respective probabilities $p$, $q$, and $1-p-q$, then player $i$'s pure strategy payoffs are
\begin{align*}
U(A_1) &= p\cdot 0 + q\cdot \bparam + (1-p-q)\cdot \bparam,\\[0mm]
U(A_2) &= p\cdot \cparam + q\cdot 1 + (1-p-q)\cdot \cparam,\\[0mm]
U(W) &= p\cdot \cparam + q\cdot \bparam + (1-p-q)\cdot \tfrac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam+\cparam-1}.
\end{align*}
For player $i$ to mix over these strategies, she must be indifferent between them. The $p$ and $q$ that equate the pure strategy payoffs are
\begin{align*}
    p &= \frac{\bparam(1-\bparam)^2}{(\bparam+\cparam-1)(\bparam^2-\bparam+\cparam^2-\cparam)},\\[6pt]
    q &= \frac{\cparam-(1-p)\bparam}{\cparam-1}.
\end{align*}
It can be shown that $p,q\in[0,1]$. By symmetry, this holds for player $-i$ as well. To obtain the equilibrium payoff, plug $p$ and $q$ into any of the above pure strategy payoffs. Consider $U(W)$.
Note that $\bparam$ and $\cparam$ are both bigger than $\tfrac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam+\cparam-1}$, the MSNE payoff of the base game. Thus, the payoff in the symmetric equilibrium of the commitment game exceeds the payoff in the base game.

\paragraph{Game of Chicken ($\bparam>1$ and $1\geq \cparam>0$)}
The commitment Game of Chicken does not have symmetric equilibria in pure strategies. We therefore only focus on mixed equilibria here. %edit
For $1\geq\cparam$, waiting weakly dominates $A_2$. Thus, in the mixed subgame perfect equilibrium there is mixing only over $A_1$ and $W$. The probability $p$ that equates $U(A_1)$ and $U(W)$ is
\[
p=\frac{\bparam^2-\bparam}{\bparam^2-\bparam+\cparam^2-\cparam+\bparam\cparam}.
\]
It can be shown that $p\in[0,1]$. Consider $U(W)$. Since $\cparam\leq1\Leftrightarrow \cparam\leq\tfrac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam+\cparam-1}$.
Thus, the commitment game symmetric equilibrium payoff is worse than the corresponding base game payoff for $\cparam<1$, except for the edge case with $\cparam=1$ where it is the same.

\paragraph{Coordination Game and Stag Hunt ($\cparam<0$ and $\bparam<1$)}
Both the Coordination Game and Stag Hunt have the symmetric equilibrium $\{A_2,A_2\}$ yielding payoff $(1,1)$ in their base game version.
This equilibrium is also achievable in a commitment game. Thus, commitment does not introduce new undominated symmetric equilibria.

\paragraph{Dominant Strategies}
Similarly, when $A_1$ or $A_2$ is a (weakly) dominant strategy in the base game, there is little to explore.
In equilibrium, players can commit to their dominant strategy, they can wait before playing it or they can mix between committing to it and waiting and then playing it.
Commitment introduces no new equilibria.

\paragraph{$\boldsymbol{A=D}$}
So far, we derived symmetric mixed equilibria for the $A<D$ case. Note that the symmetric mixed equilibria for the $A=D$ case can be found by scaling $\bparam$ and $\cparam$ by $\alpha$ and taking $\alpha \to \infty$. In particular, the equilibrium mixtures $\sigma_{A=D}$ and expected payoffs $u_{A=D}$ in the $A=D$ case can be recovered from the equilibrium mixtures $\sigma_{A<D}$ and expected payoffs $u_{A<D}$ in the $A<D$ case by $\sigma_{A=D}(\bparam,\cparam) = \lim_{\alpha \to \infty} \sigma_{A<D}(\alpha \bparam,\alpha \cparam)$ and $u_{A=D}(\bparam,\cparam) = \lim_{\alpha \to \infty} \frac{1}{\alpha}\,u_{A<D}(\alpha \bparam,\alpha \cparam)$.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-theo:convergence}}\label{sec:prooftheo2}
To prove Proposition~\ref{M-theo:convergence}, we first prove two lemmas. Lemma~\ref{lem:equilibrium} derives a symmetric mixed subgame perfect equilibrium and its convergence as $T\to\infty$. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniqueness} shows that this is the unique symmetric equilibrium in the Game of Chicken and the Battle of the Sexes.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric Mixed SPNE]\label{lem:equilibrium}
    Consider the general symmetric $2\times2$ game with parameters satisfying $\bparam>1$ and $\cparam>0$. There is a symmetric mixed subgame perfect Nash equilibrium with the strategy profile described in Table~\ref{tab:lemma4-probabilities}. Table~\ref{M-tab:convergence} of the main paper describes the convergence of this equilibrium as $T\to\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose that player $-i$ mixes across strategies $A_{1t}$, $A_{2t}$ and $W$ with probabilities $p_t$, $q_t$ and $\sigma(W)$. In the Battle of the Sexes and the Game of Chicken, the pure strategy payoffs for player $i$ are then
\begin{align}
U(A_{1t})
&= \sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\left( p_\tau \cparam + q_\tau \bparam \right)
 + p_t \cdot 0 + q_t \cdot \bparam \notag\\
&\quad + \left(1 - \sum_{\tau=1}^{t}(p_\tau + q_\tau)\right) \bparam
\label{eq:U_A1t} \\[6pt]
U(A_{2t})
&= \sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}\left( p_\tau \cparam + q_\tau \bparam \right)
 + p_t \cparam + q_t \cdot 1 \notag\\
&\quad + \left(1 - \sum_{\tau=1}^{t}(p_\tau + q_\tau) \right) \cparam
\label{eq:U_A2t} \\[6pt]
U(W)
&= \sum_{\tau=1}^{T} \left( p_\tau \cparam + q_\tau \bparam \right) \notag\\
&\quad + \sigma(W) \left( \frac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam + \cparam - 1} \right)
\label{eq:U_W}
\end{align}
To illustrate, suppose player $i$ plays $A_{1t}$. With probability $\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}(p_\tau + q_\tau)$, player $-i$ commits to $A_1$ or $A_2$ before her, and player $i$ best responds, receiving $\cparam$ or $\bparam$ accordingly.\footnote{We adopt the convention that $\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1} (\cdots) = 0$ when $t=1$, i.e.\ the sum is empty and thus vanishes.}
With probability $p_t + q_t$, both players commit simultaneously: if $-i$ also commits $A_1$, the payoff is $0$; if $-i$ commits $A_2$, player $i$ receives $\bparam$. With the remaining probability $1 - \sum_{\tau=1}^{t}(p_\tau + q_\tau)$, player $-i$ was planning to move only after player $i$ committed $A_1$. In this case, player $i$ commits, player $-i$ best responds with $A_2$ and $i$ receives the payoff $\bparam$.

\textbf{Battle of the Sexes: $\cparam>1$.}
In the symmetric mixed equilibrium, it holds that a player is indifferent between all her strategies:
\[
\begin{aligned}
U(A_{11}) &= \dots = U(A_{1T})\\
&= U(A_{21}) = \dots = U(A_{2T}) = U(W).
\end{aligned}
\]
These indifference conditions can be rearranged as follows:
\begin{align}
U(A_{11}) &= U(A_{21})
&&\Longleftrightarrow \quad q_1 = \frac{p_1 \bparam +\cparam-\bparam}{\cparam-1}
\label{eq:initial} \\
%
U(A_{1t}) &= U(A_{1,t+1})
&&\Longleftrightarrow \quad p_{t+1} = p_t\cdot\frac{\cparam}{\bparam}
\label{eq:action1} \\
%
U(A_{2t}) &= U(A_{2,t+1})
&&\Longleftrightarrow \quad q_{t+1} = q_t\cdot\frac{\bparam-1}{\cparam-1}
\label{eq:action2} \\
%
U(A_{1T}) &= U(W)
&&\Longleftrightarrow \quad \sigma(W)
= p_T\cdot\frac{\cparam}{\bparam} \notag\\
&&&\qquad {}\cdot\frac{\bparam+\cparam-1}{\bparam-1}
\label{eq:terminal1} \\
%
U(A_{2T}) &= U(W)
&&\Longleftrightarrow \quad \sigma(W)
= q_T \cdot \frac{\bparam-1}{\cparam-1} \notag\\
&&&\qquad {}\cdot\frac{\bparam+\cparam-1}{\cparam}
\label{eq:terminal2}
\end{align}
We now want to express $p_t$, $q_t$ and $\sigma(W)$ as closed form solutions in terms of $\bparam$, $\cparam$ and $T$. First note that Equations~\eqref{eq:action1} and \eqref{eq:action2} can be used to write
\[
p_t = p_1 \left(\frac{\cparam}{\bparam}\right)^{t-1}, \qquad
q_t = q_1 \left(\frac{\bparam-1}{\cparam-1}\right)^{t-1}.
\]
Next, we will use these to plug into Equations~\eqref{eq:terminal1} and \eqref{eq:terminal2} to obtain
\[
\frac{p_1}{\bparam-1} \left(\frac{\cparam}{\bparam}\right)^{T}
= \frac{q_1}{\cparam} \left(\frac{\bparam-1}{\cparam-1}\right)^{T}.
\]
Next, we plug in Equation~\eqref{eq:initial} for $q_1$ to obtain an expression that can be rearranged for $p_1$. This finally yields
\begin{equation}
   p_1 = \frac{\cparam-\bparam}{\cparam\frac{\cparam-1}{\bparam-1}\left(\frac{\cparam(\cparam-1)}{\bparam(\bparam-1)}\right)^{T}-\bparam}.
   \label{eq:p_deriv}
\end{equation}
We have thus derived a closed form expression for $p_1$, and using Equations~\eqref{eq:initial}--\eqref{eq:terminal2} we can obtain closed form expressions for all $p_t$, $q_t$ and $\sigma(W)$. Next, let's analyze the equilibrium as $T\to\infty$. Define $r=\frac{\cparam(\cparam-1)}{\bparam(\bparam-1)}$. Then
\begin{align*}
p_1 &= \frac{\cparam - \bparam}
{\cparam \frac{\cparam-1}{\bparam-1}r^T-\bparam},\\
q_1 &= \frac{p_1 \bparam + \cparam - \bparam}{\cparam - 1},\\
\sigma(W) &= p_1\left(\frac{\cparam}{\bparam}\right)^T
\frac{\bparam + \cparam - 1}{\bparam-1}.
\end{align*}
As $T\to\infty$, we distinguish three cases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item If $\cparam<\bparam$, then $r<1$, and
    \[
    p_1 \to \frac{\bparam-\cparam}{\,\bparam},\qquad q_1 \to 0,\qquad \sigma(W) \to 0.
    \]
    \item If $\cparam>\bparam$, then $r>1$, and
    \[
    p_1 \to 0,\qquad q_1 \to \frac{\cparam-\bparam}{\,\cparam-1},\qquad \sigma(W) \to 0.
    \]
    \item Finally, if $\cparam=\bparam$
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
    p_1&=\frac{1}{T+1}\frac{\bparam-1}{2\bparam-1},&
    q_1&=\frac{1}{T+1}\frac{\bparam}{2\bparam-1},\\
    \sigma(W)&\to 0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]
\end{itemize}
Note that the case $\bparam=\cparam$ is not derived from Equation~\eqref{eq:p_deriv}. Instead, we plug Equations~\eqref{eq:initial}--\eqref{eq:terminal2} into $\sum_{t=1}^T(p_t+q_t)+\sigma(W)=1$ to obtain closed form expressions. To see the equilibrium payoff in all cases consider $U(W)$. As can be seen, it converges to $\min\{\bparam,\cparam\}$ as $T\to\infty$.

\textbf{Game of Chicken: $\cparam\leq1$.}
Here, committing to $A_2$ is weakly dominated by waiting. Thus $q_t=0$ for all $t$ and the mixed equilibrium is only characterized by Equations~\eqref{eq:action1} and \eqref{eq:terminal1}.
Let $\rho=\frac{\cparam}{\bparam}$. Since the probability sequence must satisfy $ \sum_{\tau=1}^{T} p_\tau + \sigma(W) = 1 $, we can write
$$
p_1\sum_{\tau=0}^{T-1} \rho^\tau + p_1\,\rho^{T}\,\frac{\bparam+\cparam-1}{\bparam-1} = 1.
$$
The sum of the geometric series is $\sum_{\tau=0}^{T-1} \rho^\tau = \frac{1-\rho^{T}}{1-\rho}$. Plugging this into the above expression and solving for $p_1$ gives
\[
p_1 = \frac{1}{\frac{1-\rho^{T}}{1-\rho} + \rho^{T}\,\frac{\bparam+\cparam-1}{\bparam-1}},
\]
which together with Equations~\eqref{eq:action1} and \eqref{eq:terminal1} give the closed form expressions for all $p_t$ and $\sigma(W)$.
Again, let us analyze the equilibrium as $T\to\infty$. Note that $\rho^{T}\to 0$, so that the geometric series simplifies to $\frac{1-\rho^{T}}{1-\rho}\to \frac{1}{1-\rho}$. Thus,
\[
p_1\to 1-\rho,\qquad \sigma(W) \to 0.
\]
Consider $U(W)$ again to see that the equilibrium payoff converges to $\cparam$. Thus for $\cparam<1$ the equilibrium payoff worsens as $T$ increases. Note that $\cparam=1$ is an edge case, where the MSNE base game payoff $\frac{\bparam\cparam}{\bparam+\cparam-1}=1$ and thus the commitment game equilibrium payoff stays $1$ as $T\to\infty$. 

\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Unique Interior MSNE]\label{lem:uniqueness}
Consider the general form for symmetric $2\times2$ games with parameters $\bparam > 1$ and $\cparam > 0$. The strategy profile described in Table~\ref{tab:lemma4-probabilities} is the unique symmetric equilibrium in this game. 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    In the Battle of the Sexes and the Game of Chicken, each player's best response is to choose the strategy opposite to the opponent's. Thus, there cannot be a symmetric equilibrium in pure strategies.
    Therefore, we only have to prove that other than the equilibrium described in Table~\ref{tab:lemma4-probabilities},
    there is no symmetric equilibrium in mixed strategies in the commitment game. Since the equilibrium from Table~\ref{tab:lemma4-probabilities} has players mixing over all strategies in all periods with positive probability, in another equilibrium both players would have to not play some strategy with positive probability, thus at least one of $p_t,q_t$ or $\sigma(W)$ must equal zero in some period. We will now show that such a strategy profile cannot be a mixed equilibrium.

For now, consider the case $\cparam > 1$.
From Equations~\eqref{eq:U_A1t}--\eqref{eq:U_W}
it follows that
\begin{align*}
    U(A_{1t})-U(A_{1,t+1}) &= p_t \cdot (-\cparam) + p_{t+1} \cdot \bparam \\
    U(A_{2t})-U(A_{2,t+1}) &=  q_t \cdot (1-\bparam) + q_{t+1} \cdot (\cparam-1)
\end{align*}
Suppose $p_t=0$ and $p_{t+1}>0$. Then it follows that $U(A_{1t})>U(A_{1,t+1})$. Thus if one player does not play $A_{1t}$ but $A_{1,t+1}$ with positive probability, the other will want to deviate to $A_{1t}$. The same holds for $A_{2t}$ and $q_t$. Next, consider

\begin{align*}
U(A_{1T}) - U(W) &= p_{T} \cdot (- \cparam) + \sigma(W) \cdot \left( \frac{\bparam(\bparam-1)}{\bparam+\cparam-1} \right) \\
U(A_{2T}) - U(W) &= q_{T} \cdot (1-\bparam) + \sigma(W) \cdot \left( \frac{\cparam(\cparam-1)}{\bparam+\cparam-1} \right)
\end{align*}
Suppose $p_{T}>0$ and $\sigma(W)=0$. Then it follows that $U(W)>U(A_{1T})$. If one player does not wait with positive probability, the other will want to deviate to waiting. The reverse also holds. If one player's $\sigma(W)>0$, but one of $p_T$ or $q_T$ equals zero, the other player wants to deviate to that strategy. Extending this logic shows that as long as one of $p_t,q_t$ or $\sigma(W)$ equals zero, one player will always want to deviate by putting a positive probability on the corresponding strategy. Thus, the only equilibrium where both players mix over committing and waiting is such that both players mix over all strategies $\{A_{1t}, A_{2t}, W\}_{t=1}^{T}$ with positive probability. This concludes the proof for $\cparam>1$.

When $\cparam\leq1$, playing any $A_{2t}$ is weakly dominated by waiting: Equations~\eqref{eq:U_A2t} and \eqref{eq:U_W} imply $U(A_{2t})\leq U(W)$.
Players will not put probability weight on $A_2$ and thus all $q_t=0$. The argument of the proof for $\cparam>1$ then holds for $A_1$ and $W$. In equilibrium, players will mix across all strategies in the set $\{A_{1t}, W\}_{t=1}^{T}$. This concludes the proof.
\end{proof}
We have now proved parts 1 and 2 of Proposition~\ref{M-theo:convergence}. The third part of Proposition~\ref{M-theo:convergence} follows directly from arguments made in the proof of Proposition~\ref{M-theo:GamePayoffs}. $\{A_2,A_2\}$ stays an undominated equilibrium in the Coordination Game and Stag Hunt. Commitment introduces no new dynamic to base games with (weakly) dominated strategies. And finally, the $A=D$ case can be seen as the limit of $A<D$ by scaling $\bparam$ and $\cparam$ by $\alpha$ and then letting $\alpha$ go to infinity. This concludes the proof.
